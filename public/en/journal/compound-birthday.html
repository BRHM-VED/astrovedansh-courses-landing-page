Same reduced birth number, different compound days

AstroVedansh Journal·153 words

Updated: 17 October 2026

Two birthdays can reduce to the same birth number without becoming identical dates. Record both the compound day and its reduced value. Preserving the original number lets you examine compound-number teachings later without pretending the reduction retains every piece of information.

14 → 1+4=5; 23 → 2+3=5. Keep records as 14/5 and 23/5.

Think of this as two levels of information: the original day and its reduced number. Calculating the second does not require deleting the first. A total does not always let you reconstruct its components. Keep separate records for both birthdays while noting their shared reduced value. Then ask whether a teaching refers to the compound date or the reduced digit. Without that distinction, two different rules can hide under the same label. Preserving the input makes your later comparison clearer without claiming that either record determines a person’s life.

An identical reduced number does not establish identical lives or personalities.
