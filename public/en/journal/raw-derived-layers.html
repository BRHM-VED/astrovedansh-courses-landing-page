Separate raw digits from derived numbers

AstroVedansh Journal·166 words

Updated: 17 October 2026

Keep raw date digits and derived values in separate lists or visual layers. This shows whether a number was present in the original date or introduced by a later calculation. The distinction matters when counting repetitions, because adding a derived value changes the displayed frequency.

The raw list has one 3; a separate layer adding Bhagyank 3 would display two.

Think in transparent layers. The first contains the raw digits selected from the birth date. Another may contain a calculated number if your method explicitly adds one. Once merged without labels, their origins become difficult to recover. Mark the source of every added digit. In the example, the additional three did not appear as another digit in the original date; it came from a separate calculation. That distinction keeps later counting understandable. Before interpreting repetition, ask whether you are looking at repeated original digits or at values combined from different layers.

Do not describe a derived number as an extra digit present in the birth date.
