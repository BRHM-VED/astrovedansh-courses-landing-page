A grid omission rule is not a date-sum rule

AstroVedansh Journal·157 words

Updated: 17 October 2026

Keep the rules separate when constructing a grid and calculating a full-date number. The supplied course method omits the century digits 19 and zeros from the raw birth grid. For Bhagyank, return to the complete date. A rule for one representation does not automatically apply to another.

23-09-1996 raw grid: 2,3,9,9,6; full-date total: 39 → 3.

Make two worksheets: one for the complete-date sum and one for the raw grid used in this exercise. The omitted century portion belongs only to the declared grid convention. It must remain in the full-date calculation. Different totals are therefore not automatically contradictory; they answer different questions. Check the rules before checking whether the sums should match. This worked example specifies a 1996 birth year. Do not silently generalise an unstated century-handling rule to other years. Obtain the explicit course method instead of filling the gap with an assumption.

The raw-grid sum is 29; it is not the complete date total.
