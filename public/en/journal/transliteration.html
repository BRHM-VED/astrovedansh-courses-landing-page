Fix the transliteration before using an English letter table

AstroVedansh Journal·150 words

Updated: 17 October 2026

When applying an English letter table to a Hindi name, the chosen spelling matters. Similar pronunciation can be represented by different letters. Record the spelling actually used and list alternatives separately, so readers can understand why the calculations differ without assuming one is automatically superior.

Under the repeating 1–9 table, RAM=14→5; RAAM=15→6.

An unfixed transliteration can produce several calculations for what sounds like the same name. Confirm the written form first and ask which spelling is actually used. Choosing a shorter form in one calculation and a longer form in another to obtain a preferred result changes the input. Here, both spellings simply illustrate the difference. Questions about pronunciation, recognition and consistency across documents remain practical considerations of their own. Keep them separate from the numerical result instead of treating one transliteration as automatically superior because its total is different.

A transliteration-driven difference does not make one spelling inherently auspicious.
