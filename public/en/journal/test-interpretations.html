Look for counterexamples when studying interpretations

AstroVedansh Journal·153 words

Updated: 17 October 2026

After reading a traditional interpretation, do not collect only examples that appear to fit. Record counterexamples too, using questions and comparison criteria written beforehand. This makes study more honest and helps prevent broad, flexible statements from being mistaken for established predictive rules.

If 6 of 10 examples appear to fit and 4 do not, record both counts.

Define what would count as a match before examining the outcomes. If the definition changes after every personal story, the comparison becomes difficult to interpret. Record non-matches and incomplete information as well. The purpose is not to argue about a belief but to make your own study more honest. A small personal collection cannot support a broad universal claim. Practice becomes informative when you are willing to notice evidence that does not fit your expectation, rather than preserving only the examples that make the rule sound convincing.

Selected confirming examples do not scientifically validate a rule.
